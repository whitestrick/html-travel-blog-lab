Fire Island National Seashore: A Weekend Away

Just a short ferry ride from Long Island, Fire Island National Seashore felt like a different world the moment we stepped onto the dock. The barrier island stretches for over 30 miles, and its car-free communities mean the loudest sound most mornings was the surf, not traffic. We spent our days wandering the Sunken Forest, a rare maritime holly forest tucked behind the dunes, and our evenings watching the sun drop behind the historic Fire Island Lighthouse.

What struck us most was how much the island changes from one end to the other: quiet, wooded trails give way to wide open beaches, and the wildlife refuge at Otis Pike is a haven for shorebirds and, if you're lucky, a glimpse of a white-tailed deer at dusk. If you're looking for a coastal escape that trades boardwalk crowds for trails through the dunes, this is it.
